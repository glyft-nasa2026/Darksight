Model choices, not from a source: the lunar orbit's plane is the one through the site at touchdown that holds the arrival direction (so LOI needs no plane change), crossed heading south; the wait in lunar orbit stays within half a lap of Apollo 11's; the Moon's axis is taken upright to the transfer's plane (really about 1.5° from the ecliptic's normal and 6.7° from its orbit's).

Also a model choice: the departure plane is the one inclined by the pad's latitude (a launch due east) to Earth's equator that holds the arrival; of the two, the one nearer the Moon's orbit plane. Earth's pole is taken as ICRF's z (J2000; precession since then, about 0.3°, ignored). The approach's 20 m/s sink limit, its cubic height profile and half-thrust braking are the model's choices.

Updated (branch `issue-192-earth-c`): the departure plane is now the one through the pad at liftoff and the arrival point, not one inclined by the pad's latitude; the launch time is set by a search over the parking stay, half a lap to 3.5 laps (Apollo 11 parked about 1.7 laps: NASA SP-4029, cited for `APOLLO_11_TIMES`), taking the least inclined orbit. Earth's orientation is `sky.ts` `earthFromIcrf` (IAU 1976 precession and IAU 1982 sidereal time, cited in issue #80's sources); Earth is a sphere (geocentric latitude taken as the pad's). The bracketed Newton solve in `propagate` is the usual safeguard (Numerical Recipes' rtsafe idea), written here, not copied. The landing's last-150 m drift (the low gate's sink rate, falling with height) is a model choice. `ascentDvFor`'s cos(inclination) is spherical trigonometry worked out here (cos i = cos latitude × sin azimuth), checked by its test against the pad's own speed due east.
